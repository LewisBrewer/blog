The Whenua post and the three human-map posts each covered one layer of the game’s data. The map viewer puts them together. It shows Aotearoa in January 1810, the month the game opens, and the Map link at the top of the blog opens it.

What you can see

  • Whenua: forest, swamp, fern and open ground, with the rivers and lakes.
  • Settlements: pā, kāinga and anchorages.
  • Hapū territories: the land each hapū held in 1810, grown out from the places it controlled.
  • Landmarks: maunga and other named places that stand out from the terrain.
  • Tracks: walking tracks, river routes, sea lanes and portages.

Click a place to see its type, its hapū and iwi, the rangatira who held it, a population estimate from the game’s model, and the sources behind it. Search finds a place by name.

The map is a working draft. Places I haven’t confirmed carry a mark, and so do positions I’ve estimated. Places that wait on consultation have a red edge. I’d rather show the gaps than hide them. If you know a place better than the map does, let me know.
