Before modelling a single tree for Taua’s campaign map, I drew up a quick size chart for a slice of Northland. I split it into forest, scrub and coast, and lined every species up against a 45 metre scale. Kauri and kahikatea sit at the top. Below them come the canopy trees, mamaku and nīkau, then mānuka, harakeke and bracken. Tī kōuka, pōhutukawa and toetoe hold the coast.

The chart showed me a big problem straight away. If I built every species at every age for every biome, I’d be buried in assets. And on a campaign map, nobody zooms in far enough to tell a pūriri from a taraire. So I grouped them into 4 classes and then merged as many as possible, which cut the plan from 45 models to around 18ish.

Hero assets
Heroes stand about twice as tall as the canopy, so they poke out through the top of the forest. In Northland that means kauri and kahikatea. In open country or on the coast, a hero can stand alone: a pōhutukawa on a headland, or a rātā left standing in cleared ground.

Heroes will also matter in play. Each area will have hero slots that fill or empty as the forest changes. I haven’t settled the formula yet, but the aim is that you can count the giants and see how much old forest a region has left, so at a glance you can see how rich a Kauri forest is in a particular area and how much has been milled.

Canopy
The canopy makes up most of the forest, and it’s generic on purpose. Five clusters cover the whole country: northern broadleaf, lowland podocarp, beech, coastal and montane. Each one is a set of soft, rounded blobs, there for mass and colour rather than telling species apart.

In-betweens
Mamaku, nīkau and tī kōuka don’t fit either group. They’re too short to be heroes, and their shapes are too distinctive to melt into a canopy blob. You can recognise a tree fern crown or a cabbage tree’s tufted head from a long way off, and both say Aotearoa. They get their own small class that jutts out of forest canopys, and tī kōuka also dots open ground.

Ground clutter
Clutter dresses up the open ground with toetoe, mānuka, harakeke and bracken. It breaks up flat terrain and gives scrub and coast their own texture.

These are quick 3D tests in Blender. I’m checking how the canopy reads when it clumps together, with taller trees rising out of it.

Notes from the brainstorm:

  • Rimu and tōtara are new heroes I’ve suggested for forests outside Northland. Rimu still sits in the Northland canopy blob as well.
  • Some species share an asset and differ only by texture set. Just as trees have different texture sets so to can species. Ponga uses the mamaku model, kānuka uses the mānuka model, and southern rātā uses the northern rātā model.
  • Gaps worth filling later:
    • Raupō and tussock, since the clutter has nothing for wetland or high country yet.
    • Kaikawaka as a montane hero.
    • Fruit trees for 1810–1872 garden land. Fact-check how widespread they were before using them. Might be able to use a scaled down canopy tree.
